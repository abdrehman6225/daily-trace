Why RL post-training works

Evidence is building that RL post-training mostly reshapes the model's distribution over its own samples. A new paper shows that plain maximum-likelihood SFT on every archived rollout, right or wrong and without rewards, reproduces the online RL curve for Qwen3-1.7B-Base within about a point. Replaying the original algorithm on the same data collapses by step 100, and truncated importance sampling only delays that to about step 600. This fits the earlier result that 0/1-reward REINFORCE is rejection-sampling fine-tuning, the coverage-collapse proof and its KL-reset fix, and Caltech's ROAM-GRPO diversity reward. Watch for whether the SFT-replay result holds at larger scale and on agentic tasks.

Looped transformers are moving from releases into architecture and infrastructure fixes for their cost. Reward design now covers per-step credit, whether reward labels are correct, and calibrated stopping. NTU's RLVB uses a simulator that can enumerate hidden states to reward each step from the exact posterior. That stops vision-language agents from answering when evidence is insufficient, which base models do on 3–68% of such turns, but the gains come almost entirely from waiting longer rather than reasoning better. Linear and hybrid attention

Cheaper attention is now standard in open agent models, but retrieval costs are rarely reported. inclusionAI's Ling-3.2-air (124B/5.5B active) mixes 35 KDA linear layers with 7 Gated MLA layers (5:1). It reports AutomationBench-AA rising from 15.7% to 61% and Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 0% to 27%. Baidu's ERNIE 5.5 Flash (280B/14B active, 1M context) instead compresses KV to one entry per 128 tokens in 37 layers and uses sparse attention in 6. Allen-Zhu's 64 runs showed linear models fail in-context retrieval that standard benchmarks miss, and FlashMorph2 and Clover pick which layers keep full attention. Watch for retrieval-specific evals on these releases.

Code retrieval for agents

Evidence keeps favoring structure and lexical search over plain embeddings for coding agents. Ant Group's RepoPremium shows dense search's lead over BM25 mostly comes from benchmark setup. graphify builds a tree-sitter graph with no embeddings. CodeGraph, a local MCP server, indexes symbols, call edges and dependencies into SQLite. Over 7 repos with Opus 4.8 it reports 88% fewer tool calls, 62% fewer tokens and 44% lower cost, but these are the author's own benchmarks. Watch for independent comparisons of graph, BM25 and hybrid retrieval on real agent tasks.
